The three layers

The mesh tunnel is WireGuard, with ChaCha20-Poly1305, between two machines. The data fabric link is TLS 1.3 between a program and a router, and it prefers a hybrid post-quantum key exchange. Sealing for named readers uses hybrid encryption, and the platform holds no key.

The layers cover different spans. Sealing runs from the owner to the readers. The link and the tunnel each run between two ends on the owner's own machines.

Assumptions

Light in optical fibre covers about 200 km per millisecond. A real route is about 1.5 times the straight-line distance. The two machines are 10 km apart, a regional relay is 500 km away, and the distant gateway is 6,700 km away.

A middle stop that opens, inspects and seals traffic again costs about 1 to 2 ms. A satellite hop in low orbit adds about 10 to 20 ms. The far entry point is about 9,000 km away.

With real links

A lost packet costs at least one more round trip to repair. When a timer has to run out first, the wait is set from the round-trip time and its variation, and it doubles with each repeated loss (RFC 6298).

One connection sustains roughly 1.22 × packet size ÷ (round-trip time × the square root of the loss rate), after Mathis and others, 1997. With 1 percent loss and 1,460-byte packets that is about 70 megabits per second at 2 ms and about 0.3 megabits per second at 450 ms.

Conversational voice is planned for about 150 ms one way (ITU-T G.114). Anything that expects an answer within a fixed short time fails once delay and retries pass that limit.

Stacked overlays

Encryption is not the cost: one processor core seals or opens on the order of 1 to 3 gigabytes per second. Each tunnel adds roughly 50 to 80 bytes to a 1,500-byte packet, so three stacked tunnels use more than a tenth of every packet.

Eight stops that are each up 99.9 percent of the time give a path that is up about 99.2 percent, roughly 70 hours down in a year. Here the layers are applied at the ends only: six seal-or-open operations, and none in the middle.

Satellite links commonly give a terminal no public address and accept no inbound connection. The mesh needs neither, because every machine connects outward.

Why a relay in a cloud is safe

A relay passes connections that are encrypted from end to end by the two parties that hold the keys. It holds no key, it cannot read or change what it carries, and it stores nothing.

A relay can be replaced or moved at any time. Losing one loses no data and no identity.

What an assessor can read

The declaration states the machines, the exact version of everything by content hash, the connections that are allowed, and who may do what. Every change is a signed, reviewed commit. Deploys and checks leave their own records.

A collector on every machine gathers the system journal, the audit log, the data fabric's audit records and the access logs. It normalizes them to OCSF and sends them off the machine. A forwarder to an organization's own collector is switched on by setting one address. Services also emit events over OpenTelemetry.

Vulnerability scans (ACAS), configuration baselines (STIG) and web application testing (Burp) are outside tools that an organization still runs.

The two boundary nodes

Federation. goatbuilder federate <partner> sets up a peering in one run and can back it out. Each side keeps its own environment and authority. Recognizing the other side is a separate check from authorizing it.

Simple. A dedicated machine sends released data as one plain UDP or TCP stream, one way, to one known destination. It meets the other side's encrypted gateway, and it stops sending if the tunnel is down. It is for a system that cannot join a data fabric.

What an air gap does and does not do

A working system is rarely cut off for long. Data, updates and people have to get in and out, so the gap is crossed by hand, and those crossings are the least watched doors.

Isolation slows updates, so the inside falls behind on fixes. It gives no help once something is inside.

A documented case: in 2008 a compromise of classified United States military networks "began when an infected flash drive was inserted into a U.S. military laptop", and the code "spread undetected on both classified and unclassified systems", as the Deputy Secretary of Defense wrote (W. Lynn, Foreign Affairs, September 2010).

It suits a thing that changes rarely and talks to no one, such as a key kept offline. It does not suit a system of many connected parts that exchange data all the time.

The race of clocks: sources

The clocks measured here are this project's own measurements. Every other value is from a published report, with its year. They measure different populations in different ways, so compare their size, not their last digit.

Why the attacker's clocks moved: systems used to be disconnected, knowledge of a flaw spread slowly, and the tools needed a career expert or a state. Now systems are connected, a flaw is published and automated within days, and capable tools are widely available.

  • Average time to exploit a flaw: Mandiant, "How Low Can You Go? An Analysis of 2023 Time-to-Exploit Trends", 15 October 2024: an average of 63 days in 2018 to 2019 and of five days in 2023, with outliers left out (source).
  • Average time from a first foothold to moving through the network, 2017: CrowdStrike, press release on its 2018 Global Threat Report, 26 February 2018: one hour and 58 minutes (source).
  • The same, 2025: CrowdStrike, press release on its 2026 Global Threat Report, 24 February 2026: an average of 29 minutes for criminal intrusions, the fastest in 27 seconds (source).
  • Median time inside before being noticed, 2011: Mandiant, "M-Trends 2021", 13 April 2021: a global median of 416 days reported in 2011 (source).
  • The same, 2025: Mandiant, "M-Trends 2026", 23 March 2026: a global median of 14 days, up from 11 (source).
  • Time to fix half of the critical flaws: Verizon, release on its 2024 Data Breach Investigations Report, 1 May 2024: 55 days to remediate 50 percent of critical vulnerabilities after patches are available (source).
  • Mean time to identify and contain a data breach: IBM, "2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report": a mean of 241 days (source).
  • Time to recover after ransomware: Sophos, The State of Ransomware 2024 (survey of 5,000 organizations, January to February 2024): 34 percent took more than a month (source).

Not shown: the time to lock down data and access after an incident. No defensible published number was found.

Joining

A device that joins presents a key that works once. It then gets its identity and the list of parties it may reach.

Services have names that exist only inside the goatnet. The service that admitted a device is not in the path of that device's traffic afterward.

The four checks

CheckWhat it means
Who is askingA person, a machine and a program each prove their identity in their own way.
Is there a ruleA connection needs a declared rule. With no rule there is no connection.
Is it allowedA declared table says what each role may do.
Is the identity still goodAn identity can be revoked, and a machine can be held for review.

The rules in force are generated from a declaration that is reviewed like code. A partner's identities can be verified without being granted anything.

Sealing for several readers

The owner seals a data set once. The key that opens it is then sealed separately for each named reader, with that reader's own key.

So several readers can open the same data, each with a different key. A reader can be added or removed without changing anyone else's key.

The platform holds no key for sealed data. The data fabric that carries it holds no key and has no central broker.

Post-quantum key exchange

The routers of the data fabric prefer the combined key exchange. A client that does not support it connects the usual way.

For mesh tunnels a post-quantum key exchange is built and not enabled. Its standardized replacement is being selected.

A site that is cut off

A site that is cut off keeps admitting people, enrolling machines and changing access. This part is designed; the tests for mesh control and sign-in under a cut link are built.

A site that sends nothing off-site gives less to find in the radio spectrum, to signals collection and on the network.
