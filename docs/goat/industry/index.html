Learn goat, run it and connect your product to it. Then talk to us, with it working.

goat is an open-source data fabric: an encrypted mesh, identity for people, machines and workloads, and publish/subscribe data, built from open standards. It connects sensors, effectors, C2 systems and AI across organizations and nations. Everything a company needs to integrate a product is public, and this page is the route from here to a working integration.

For business development

The first meeting comes after a working integration

We do not take sales meetings to learn about a product. goat is public, its interfaces are open standards, and the fastest way to show where a product belongs is to connect it. Send this page to the engineer who would build that connection. When it runs, we want to hear from you.

What goat is

One system that does three jobs: an encrypted WireGuard mesh between every device, identity for people, machines and workloads, and Zenoh publish/subscribe with no central broker. Data moves by who you are, not where you sit, and keeps moving when links to the cloud are cut.

Who runs it

U.S. and allied forces in Europe run goat today, connecting their sensors and systems across national lines. Each nation runs its own environment and shares data by deliberate release. Systems from many companies already publish onto it.

What your company gets

A product that works on the fabric those forces use, shown working before anyone meets. A listing in awesome-goat, the public list of work built on goat. Engineers who know the current open stack. No fee, no contract and no approval needed to start.

For the engineer

Five steps from nothing to a listed integration

You need a Linux or macOS laptop, and for step 2 one Ubuntu 24.04 host or VM with 4 cores and 16 GB. No account with any outside service is needed: the container images are public. Each step says what it takes and what you have at the end.

A coding agent reads goat well. Point it at llms.txt, a map of the system written for agents, and at goatcore, goatenv and goatdocs, each of which carries an AGENTS.md for agents.

Standards

goat is assembled from standards industry already chose

Nothing in goat asks a company to adopt a proprietary protocol or platform. Each layer is an open standard or a widely used open-source project, and each can be replaced. A team that knows these already knows most of goat.

LayerStandard or projectWhere else it is used
Encrypted tunnelsWireGuardPart of the Linux kernel; the tunnel under most modern VPN products
Mesh controlNetBirdAn open-source mesh VPN used by companies of every size
Data fabricEclipse ZenohAn Eclipse Foundation project, used in robotics (ROS 2), vehicles and industrial systems
Sign-in for peopleOpenID Connect and OAuth 2.0, through OryThe sign-in standard of most web services
Identity for workloadsSPIFFE and SPIREA graduated project of the Cloud Native Computing Foundation
Track and sensor dataCursor on Target (CoT), ASTERIX, SAPIENTTAK and allied C2 systems; air surveillance across Europe; counter-drone sensor integration
Hosts and deploymentUbuntu, Ansible, Docker, PostgreSQL, PrometheusStandard infrastructure in industry and government

A small change for your product

Your product keeps its own format and its own interface. goat converts at the door, and the conversion is code you can read.

No lock-in, in either direction

Every part is replaceable, including goat's own. An integration built on these standards works with other systems that use them.

The current edge of the field

Brokerless publish/subscribe, identity on every packet, post-quantum key exchange and AI agents with their own identities are all in the tree. Building on goat puts your engineers on that work, with everyone else building on it.

It can be fun

Games run on a goat fabric too. goatrodeo exercises Zenoh in public, in a browser.

License

Use it for any purpose, including a product you sell

goat is licensed under the Apache License 2.0. A company may use it, change it, build it into commercial products and services, and sell them, with no fee and no contributor agreement. Your own code stays yours: a proprietary product on goat stays proprietary.

What the license says

Apache-2.0 grants use for any purpose, modification and redistribution, with a patent grant from every contributor. Keep the license and notice files when you redistribute goat itself. The parts written by U.S. federal employees are in the public domain in the United States. Components from other projects keep their own licenses, listed in each repository's NOTICE; a few, such as the NetBird management server, are AGPL-3.0.

Dual use by design

Nothing in goat is specific to one mission. The same fabric connects sensors and systems for emergency services, utilities, ports, industrial sites, research networks and disaster response as it does for defense. A company can sell what it builds on goat to any of these customers.
